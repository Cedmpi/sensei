This is Dr. Bernstein with another edition of Dr. Bernstein's Diabetes University.
This time we're going to talk about diabetic pregnancies.

Diabetic pregnancies are notorious for their effects upon the pregnant woman and also
their effects upon the fetus, upon fetal development and fetal size. Most
diabetic pregnancies involve very high blood sugars and also involve obesity of
the mother because the doctors are pushing high carbohydrates for these
mothers to be. 

There are a number of complications that occur during these
pregnancies, again due to the high carbohydrate and high blood sugars,
namely very high blood pressure and complications of the high blood pressure
that can lead to loss of consciousness and even death of the mother.

Because organogenesis, namely the creation of new organs, begins during the
first trimester of pregnancy, the first three months, we see a high incidence of
impaired organ development in children of diabetic mothers, again due to the
high blood sugars. Now to give you an example of some of the things that can
happen, I had one patient who wanted to get pregnant. She got pregnant, by the way
against my advice, because she had not yet controlled her own overeating and
her obstetrician was one of the many who advocated great weight gain and high
carbohydrate diets, making ridiculous claims like the baby needs a lot of
carbohydrate, as if he cannot get the carbohydrate from the mother's blood
sugar, whatever it happens to be.

He also ignored the fact that the average blood
sugar of non-diabetic women, non-obese non-diabetic women who become pregnant, at
least during the first half of pregnancy, the blood sugars run around 65. That's
normal blood sugar. They don't feel hypoglycemic and they feel fine with
blood sugars of 65, but this was ignored. The lady was told to eat the ADA
recommendation of 137 grams of carbohydrate a day and the child was
born with cystic fibrosis. 

I had resigned from the case as soon as the
obstetrician required the high carbohydrate diet. I said either we go to
a low-carb obstetrician who believes in normal blood sugars or I cannot be in
charge of your diabetes because I don't know how to control your blood sugars
with all that carbohydrate. A sad ending, but on the other side of the coin we
have an endocrinologist who specializes in diabetic pregnancies.

Her name is Lois Jovanovich and she is the medical director of the SANSUM,
Center in Santa Barbara, California, and she has been specializing in normal
blood sugars for pregnant women for many years, I would say about 40 years, well at
least 35 years, and she is famous for the fact that her mothers don't develop
complications and the offspring do not develop complications. She gets normal
babies far more than anyone else simply by helping her mothers to get normal
blood sugars.
