### Closed-loop insulin pumps?
*What are your thoughts on the news today from Medtronic regarding the approval of the closed-loop insulin pump? Do you think this system will be able to keep blood sugars in tight enough range?*

Absolutely not. The whole concept of insulin pumps is a myth.
The myth is that you set it for certain basal doses, which is reasonable,
but then that you guess at the amount of carbohydrate that you're going to get in a meal
and set a bolus to cover that amount of carbohydrate.

And that you can change the
amount of carbohydrate from one meal to the next, from one day to the next, and so on.
And basically, it's telling you to guess at a dose and hope that it works. And that's asking
for trouble.

On top of that, it's only looking at carbohydrate, where if you read my book,
you'll know that protein also affects blood sugar and that the mass or bulk of the food
affects blood sugar. So you can eat a non-food and raise your blood sugar. For example,
a type 1 diabetic can eat a handful of pebbles and his blood sugar will go up when it distends
the gut.

So the whole concept is based on dietary myths, but there are other myths and there are
other problems. For example, the fact that eventually you develop scar tissue around the
infusion site and that makes the absorption of insulin unpredictable.

So here you have something that's guaranteed not to work and yet
it gets approved by the FDA as if it's going to be a magical solution.
