The subject is going to be diabetes and depression. I receive many professional
diabetes journals and they're about oh I'd say 30 or 40 articles in each
journal. I get them every month so I probably see several hundred articles
per month and there are always every month several articles on diabetes and
depression documenting the very high depression scores amongst people with
diabetes.

And there are studies constantly trying to correlate
depression with race, with where in the world you live, with other things that
are not likely to really cause depression, with how old you were when
you developed diabetes, etc. About 35 years ago I conducted two studies at two
universities. This was when I was an engineer before I became a physician.

These studies led to my wanting to be a physician because we couldn't sell the
medical profession on controlling blood sugars and the purpose of the studies
were to see if we could reverse diabetic complications with normal blood sugars.

And indeed we showed that we could but when I was recruiting the patients I had
to go to physicians who were members of the New York chapter of the American
Diabetes Association and I had to talk them into donating their, what
they considered were their worst patients, the patients with the most
erratic blood sugars and those they considered the least cooperative and
they told me you're going to make them crazy but I'm anxious to get rid of this
patient so we'll give them to you.

So here we had two university groups
working with these hopeless patients and I knew from my own feelings after I
controlled my blood sugar that we had to document that we're not going to drive
them crazy in fact we're going to make them better and we hired, we didn't pay
them but we recruited, a psychiatrist for studying one group and a
psychologist for studying the other group and they both administered the
Hamilton Depression Score which is a classic test for putting a number on the
severity of depression.

Under 6 is no depression and over 6 is depressed and in these groups
we managed to be over 20 in both groups at the start of the study. At the ends of
the study, through two different studies, two different groups, we were under six
for the people we were working with. So here we went from severely depressed to
not depressed and one of the psychiatric results got published in a major journal
they published it without using my name because I was already taking pre-med
courses and had vanished and they merely showed the difference that there was a
big improvement but they didn't interview the patients to or they didn't
publish the interviews with the patients to explain why.

I interviewed all of the
patients and I got the same answer from each patient. They all said that my blood
sugars were varying from 20 to 800 every day, the highs and lows were hitting me
at random, I didn't know where they were coming from, I had no control over this, I
never knew what each day would have in store for me, I was walking a tightrope
and some people said they were walking over a knife edge and almost every one
of them used the same words to describe the conclusion. Now I'm captain of my own
ship, I can make my blood sugar whatever I want it to be, I'm running the
show and that made sense.

That was my personal experience because I was
depressed, I felt I was living in a prison and when I first got the idea of
telling the world about how to normalize blood sugar, remember I was the first the
only person measuring his own blood sugars with a blood sugar meter. I think
we have one of them up here. This is what I carried around with me in a
shoulder bag and I was the only one in the world doing this and I was able to
achieve normal blood sugars.

Of course it only worked with a very low carbohydrate
diet and I had to take multiple shots of insulin because I'm a type 1 diabetic
but I felt I had been, I had escaped from a prison and then I thought gee there
are other people out there just like me and they have no chance, they're
locked in.

So I tried to persuade the medical profession that everyone should
be doing what I was doing. It was total failure, no one was interested and I
ended up going to medical school so I could be published. But it all started
with my being depressed and the depressed being raised by being put in
charge of my own blood sugars.

Now this is described in detail in an article, that
I actually wrote as a required article for our course in psychiatry when I was
in medical school and when they said you have to write a paper to pass this
course I right away thought of this subject, wrote the paper and after I
graduated and had a little time available I got the paper published and
we have a link that you can find below to this article which was published in
the journal of the Israel Psychiatric Association and you can print it and
show it to people and it's all there so please take a look and try to understand
that controlling blood sugar following a low carbohydrate diet does not make you
depressed it does exactly the opposite.
