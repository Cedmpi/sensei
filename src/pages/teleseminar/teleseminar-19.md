is a measure of the amount that a certain food will raise blood sugar in a non-diabetic
compared to bread, which by the way raises blood sugar more than pure glucose.

So they take a ratio of the blood sugar increase in a non-diabetic. But most non-diabetics,
especially with the small amounts of food that they use in checking the glycemic index,
non-diabetics are not going to show much of a blood sugar increase.

Yet with a very small amount of the foods that they're testing, I will show a huge blood sugar
increase because I'm diabetic. So where peanuts supposedly have a very low glycemic index of 15%,
a bag of peanuts will raise my blood sugar by something like 200 milligrams per deciliter,
which is a huge amount even though they say it has a low glycemic index.

So it's a meaningless
number when it comes to diabetics. And glycemic load is a measure of the total carbohydrate
in a product, I believe unrelated to blood sugar. So all you have to do is look at the
total carbohydrate content and you don't have to look at a number that's been calculated
by different formulas by different people.
