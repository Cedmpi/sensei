Hi, this is Dr. Bernstein with Session 14 of Dr. Bernstein's Diabetes University.
And today we're going to talk about tooth infections or infections in the mouth.

Now why on earth would I want to talk about that? Simply because it's just so common.
Almost every diabetic patient runs into this sooner or later. In fact, when I see new patients
who are diabetic but they're new to me, virtually 100% of those over the age of 40,
actually it's 1% minus two people over the past 30 years, have had root canal infections
and root canal treatments. So significant tooth infections
are pretty darn common. And they're even common in children.

Now I want to cite a true story to show you the seriousness of this.
I had a patient who was a 10 year old boy
whose mother called me because all of a sudden his blood sugars were very high where they had
been normal for months. And I said does he have a cold, a sore throat, any kind of infection
anywhere? And she said no.

I said he's got to go to the dentist immediately. And does he have any
pain in his mouth, any toothaches? Nope. Well have the dentist tap on every tooth. He should tap on
the biting surface of the tooth and on the buckle surface with the side of the tooth because
sometimes tapping will elucidate pain or tenderness and identify the tooth with the infection.

He should also look at all of the gums to see if any gums are puffy or red because there could be
a gum in the flat infection. And as a last resort if the tapping doesn't disclose a problem and the
looking at the gums doesn't show anything, you touch each tooth with a chip of ice and
most dentists have ice crushers or some way of putting cold on a tooth.

So they took the young
man to his dentist and the dentist refused to tap on the teeth. Instead he did something that 50%
of the dentists do. He did a panoramic x-ray which at that time cost about $200 and a panoramic
x-ray will not disclose an infection in a tooth or around a tooth. But it's very profit effective
and this dentist refused to do any tapping. He did a panoramic x-ray and of course found nothing
because it's not possible to find what we're looking for.

And the father phoned me from the
dentist's office and put him on the phone. The dentist said to me he's not going to let a
non-dentist 3,000 miles away tell him how to treat his patients. I said that's fine but I can assure
you that if you don't tap this kid's teeth the entire family is going to go to a new dentist.

Well he ended up tapping the teeth, found one tooth that was tender was wisdom tooth
and he extracted the tooth and found the kid had an abscess under that tooth.
So and right away the kid's blood sugars went down. This is a typical story. I've actually
had identical stories with other children where the dentists wanted to do panoramic x-rays and
refused to do the tapping.

Now this does not just apply to kids it applies to adults also.
And frequently they need they find a tender tooth and the dentist may not know whether it's a gum
disease or a an endodontal disease like a root canal infection. So if he sends the
diabetic patient to either a periodontist who knows gums or to an endodontist who knows root
canals they are capable of making the diagnosis. 

By the way what the endodontist would do besides
looking at gums would be to stick a probe between the gum and the tooth and if it goes down more
than six or seven millimeters he knows that he has an infection. Now
well how do you take care of the infections? You either go to the endodontist, the periodontist
and the one who's appropriate for the type of infection will do the treatment.

Now how do you prevent this? First of all you should either floss after meals.
My teeth are too tight for me to floss so I keep these little plastic toothpicks handy
that have a little plastic brush at one end that fits between my teeth
and I use this after meals. In addition you should brush your teeth thoroughly twice a day
and ideally use an electric toothbrush like the Sonicare toothbrush or any one of these
electric toothbrushes. 

When you brush you go over each tooth twice using a W motion up and down
while you're moving the brush across the tooth and you should do this at least twice a day
and you should get plaque scraping by a dental technician every three months. That's really a
must. They're slightly expensive they may cost a hundred dollars, 125 dollars in the USA
but the cost of a root canal treatment or a gum treatment is so much greater
that just preventing the infections can save you a lot of money.

The plaque scraping is best done in the office of a periodontist who usually supervises,
that's a gum doctor, who usually supervises his technician and checks for cavities and so on at
the same time. So this is an important subject. It's hard to overemphasize it because almost every
patient who comes in has a history of these tooth infections and even kids will frequently get
infections especially with newly erupting teeth. 

So take good care of your teeth and we'll see you
with the next session of Dr. Bernstein's Diabetes University. Thank you.
