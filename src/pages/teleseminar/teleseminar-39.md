### Special subject: Sulfonylurea drugs

Before we start our current session, I have a few special subjects to cover,
but the first one has to do with an article that was published
in October 2018 in the [Lancet Endocrinology Diabetes Journal](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(18)30025-1/abstract).
It was an evaluation of sulfonylurea drugs for type 2 diabetes
and it posed the question that's been around for maybe 50 years,
are sulfonylurea drugs still appropriate for the treatment of diabetes?

And it was interesting that this was a very thorough summary
of the many studies of the effects of sulfonylurea drugs
and it was evaluating only two things.
Do they cause excess or severe hypoglycemia and do they cause heart disease?
Because these questions are brought up repeatedly
or have been brought up over the years.

And the conclusion was that there is no heart evidence
that sulfonylurea drugs cause heart disease
and that the risk of severe hypoglycemia is limited to certain individuals
such as the very elderly and so on.
So if you select your patients appropriately,
they were recommending that sulfonylureas are fine.

However, in the middle of the article, which was quite long,
there was an entire page listing the studies that showed
that sulfonylurea drugs stopped working after 1 to 3 years.
And when they're combined with other drugs for type 2 diabetes,
I'm not talking about insulin but I'm talking about oral agents,
it seems that all of them stopped working together
if they're used with sulfonylureas.

Now the logical conclusion to this is that sulfonylureas
probably cause beta cell burnout
and that's why they stopped working in one to three years.
I think that there was one case of four years.

Now there have been a number of other studies
that show that in animals, sulfonylurea drugs do burnout beta cells
and they even go through the mechanisms by which sulfonylurea drugs
cause beta cells to self-destruct, called apoptosis.

So if I were evaluating sulfonylureas,
I would say if you expect the patient to live more than one or two years
for at the outside, don't give him sulfonylurea drugs
because you're going to destroy his beta cell.
