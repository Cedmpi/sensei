Hi, this is Dr. Richard Bernstein bringing you Dr. Bernstein's Diabetes University.
Our subject for today is going to be diets for weight gain and weight loss.

Now, remember we're addressing the subject of diabetes, but when it comes to weight gain
and weight loss, the same principles apply to non-diabetics.

I've been doing this with patients for more than 30 years and I've seen thousands of patients
and it doesn't matter whether they're diabetic or not, the ball game is still the same. If you
want to lose weight, you must be following a low carbohydrate diet. Low fat diets don't work,
low calorie diets don't work. I'm not going to go into the science of it because I've done
this on a previous session, but the main thing is total elimination of simple sugars,
which include starches, breads, grains, fruits, juices, all the sweet stuff that you can buy in
a bag at the corner store, but there comes a time when you've achieved the weight loss that you
seek and here is where many other systems make a big mistake.

They right away start giving people
carbohydrate again so that they will not continue to lose weight, but what that does is reinstitutes
the craving for carbohydrates, the desire for sweets, the addiction to carbohydrate.

So what we do when we want to put a halt to the weight loss, we've lost enough weight now,
we increase the protein. Now if you're a diabetic and taking insulin, you're going to have to
increase the amount of insulin you take for a meal slightly to take into account the protein
that you're bringing back into the meal plan and there are crude formulas. For example,
if you're not insulin resistant and overweight, typically
one half unit of regular insulin, also called crystalline insulin, will cover about one ounce
of protein (~28 g).

So that's a good starting point. You try it and see how it works and you can raise or
lower the dose from that point, but if you're taking insulin, you're going to need more insulin
for the protein, for the greater amount of protein. If you're not taking insulin, you may or
may not need an addition of another medication. It all depends what happens to your blood sugars,
but if you're diabetic, don't expect to be adding protein to your diet with no effect
upon blood sugar. Although the effect is much smaller than a similar amount of carbohydrate,
it still exists and you have to cover it with some medication in most situations.

So this is just a reminder that you don't bring back the carbo when you think you're losing too
much weight. You add protein and in some cases, especially with small children, adding more protein
to the existing, let's say, three meals may be too much for a little kid to eat in one sitting.
So we add snacks and at the snacks, we still have to cover the protein within the new protein with
insulin. 

So there are lots of ways to play the game, but you don't go back to increasing
carbohydrate. That will bring high blood sugars, loss of muscle mass because you're going to be
converting not only your dietary protein to glucose, but also your body's protein to glucose,
peeing it away in your urine and in effect, converting your muscles to sugar water
if you bring back carbohydrate. That's the picture. I hope you enjoyed this.
We'll see you at another session.
