### Special subject: Red meat and health

Now, today we have two special subjects.

First of all, there was a meta-analysis of many studies comparing individuals who consumed more than
three servings a week of red meat versus people who avoided red meat. The results of this
meta-analysis were published in [Archives of Internal Medicine in October of 2019](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M19-1621), and they came up
with new guidelines that do not restrict the consumption of red meat in order to benefit your
health. In fact, they also point out that some people, for health reasons, perhaps should be
consuming red meat.

The warnings against red meat that are so popular in the press and on the internet apparently are
invalidated by this meta-analysis.

Now, I'm not telling people go run out and eat red meat. I'm just pointing out the results of the
meta-analysis. I personally think that the breeding of animals to then kill them is not a civilized
thing to do, but it seems to be necessary the way we're currently organized.

### Special subject: Continuous glucose monitors and finger sticks

You may have encountered cover stories on many diabetes journals, including this one from the
American Diabetes Association that says, zero finger sticks, glucose readings on my phone, total
game changer. That's an absolute lie.

Apparently the sequence of events goes something like this. There's a freestyle CGM, continuous
glucose monitor, that started advertising in competition with Dexcom and making claims that
initially Dexcom hadn't been making, namely that there were no more finger sticks if you used their
instrument, the freestyle instrument.

This was a lie because you do require finger sticks to calibrate it and also to correct it because
both instruments, any continuous glucose monitor that matches current technology has to be at least
20 minutes to a half an hour behind the actual reading and in many cases may be way off from the
actual reading.

In my experience, I have a Dexcom continuous glucose monitor. I have to do more finger sticks since
I got the glucose monitor than I did before the glucose monitor.

Why? Because it's not always accurate. My main reason for having it is to get warnings of
hypoglycemia. I would say that about half those warnings are erroneous.

If I get a warning, I get a siren alarm that can wake anybody up and the first thing I'll do will be
to check my blood sugar. Half the time it'll be a valid warning and I have to take some glucose, but
the other half of the time I have a normal blood glucose or maybe even slightly elevated.

I do more finger sticks since I got my CGM than I did before I got the CGM. Don't believe what you
see on the cover of the diabetes publications.
