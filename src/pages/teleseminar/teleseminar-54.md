### Have you heard of any researchers harvesting some of the patient's remaining beta cells, growing them in vitro, and reintroducing the newly grown beta cells, thus avoiding any need for immunosuppression from cadaver transplants? [short: Harvested beta cells regrown and transplanted]

No, I've not heard of this.

It's difficult and probably traumatic to harvest beta cells, but there are important points to note.

Beta cells survive and replicate best when they're in a natural environment, which includes what's
called the paracrine effect. Paracrine effect is the effect of hormones on one type of beta cell
that makes one type of hormone on the adjacent beta cells that make another type of hormone.

There's alpha, beta, gamma, delta, and epsilon, at least 5 different kinds of beta cells making 5 
different hormones. These hormones not only affect the metabolism of the body and blood sugar and so
on, but they affect the survival of the adjacent beta cells.

It's important to put the beta cells back where they belong. That's a very difficult job and makes
beta cell transplanting very difficult.

Another thing they need is adequate vasculature. You need nearby blood vessels that will accept
insulin from the beta cell, but will also nourish the beta cell.
