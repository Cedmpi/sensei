### Special subject: HDL

been a study conducted in Europe that had a lot of publicity in the past
couple of weeks to the effect that HDL is a u-shaped predictor of cardiac risk.

authors point out as an aside that probably will not be covered in the

also be causes of mortality.

raises HDL. So if someone's drinking too much alcohol that may affect his

statins. So there may be a number of medications and other substances that
people may be taking that raise their HDL and also raise their mortality. So we
have this tentative report that high HDL can adversely affect mortality as well
as low HDL.

### Gastroparesis 
*I believe that I may have diabetic gastroparesis, but nobody any 
closer than you seems to run RR interval tests for cardiac autonomic neuropathy.
What more commonly available lab test would you
recommend instead even if it is more expensive?*

Well yes it's true I don't
know of any physician routinely doing RR studies. It's the best test for
predicting gastroparesis. It's a measure of damage to the vagus nerve. The vagus
nerve is responsible for slowing the heart when you exhale and it's
responsible for digestion and stomach emptying.

So you're not slowing the heart
adequately when you exhale. We also know that your digestion has probably been
affected and we can put numbers on this with the RR interval study. The most
common study recommended by the medical profession is the gamma ray
camera or technetium scan where they have you swallow some radioactive eggs
or chopped liver and they take gamma ray pictures of your gastrointestinal system
especially your stomach over a period of hours and see how fast you digest the
meal.

The trouble is that the hallmark of gastroparesis is its unpredictability.
You don't know when you're going to be emptying your stomach. Today you might
empty it rapidly. Tomorrow you might take 12 hours to empty your stomach. So you go
for a technetium scan and you go today you'll show normal but if you went
tomorrow it wouldn't be normal. I found after doing these on patients that we
had to do four studies in order to get one abnormal on most patients. 

If you
have extreme gastroparesis with nausea and vomiting we might get it on the
first study. Now what alternates are there? Well I get clues just by looking
at blood sugars. If a patient is following the regimen of my book
Diabetes Solution and would therefore otherwise have normal blood sugars
around the clock and on some nights they have dinner blood sugar goes low after
dinner and then is very high the next morning that tells me that they probably
did not die promptly digested dinner we're talking about type 1 diabetics now
took insulin for the meal intended to cover the dinner but the dinner didn't
get digested and blood sugar went way down had to take glucose to bring the
sugar back up to normal and then overnight the stomach emptied and the
dinner finally raised the blood sugar but there was no rapid-acting insulin
left to cover it so just by looking at blood sugars you can draw likely
conclusions about gastroparesis.

And this is reiterated if you get variability one
night you get low then high and then the next night with the same meal you get
level after dinner and level overnight that tells me that you're emptied and
it was covered by the rapid-acting insulin you took for the dinner so
things work nicely that night and then another night they go low then high
again these are all clues to gastroparesis and of course there could
be other digestive problems than gastroparesis,

there are many that may
exist but in diabetics the most common thing is gastroparesis in fact in my
experience virtually 100% of the new type 1 patients whom I see after they've
had diabetes for five years have gastroparesis and we spot it on the
rr study in fact I spot it on the R-R study for type 2 diabetics too after
five years but because they're making their own insulin they may not show the
extreme blood sugar patterns so the only alternative alternate to an RR study
that I can think of is looking at blood sugars.
