### Does gastroparesis cause your heart rate to fluctuate? Will it cause irregular heart rate? [short: Can gastroparesis affect heart rate?]

I doubt that gastroparesis controls your heart rate, but autonomic neuropathy can cause both
gastroparesis and a more rapid heart rate.

So in fact, autonomic neuropathy reduces the variability
of heart rate. In the extreme case, people have no variation in their heart rate, even upon deep
breathing.

And it's not caused by gastroparesis, although such people usually have severe
gastroparesis because the same nerve, the vagus nerve, is the nerve that both slows the heart and
facilitates digestion.

### I read that you had frozen shoulder and both shoulders. Did they ever heal back to the way they were before the condition? How many years passed before they were functioning normally? [short: Frozen shoulders healing time?]

Frozen shoulder does not heal spontaneously. It involves glycation of collagen, which is in the
connective tissues of tendons, etc., that occur around the shoulder and in a structure called the
brachial plexus. As these tendons, when they get glycated, they shrink and tighten and you get
what's called an impingement syndrome in the brachial plexus, where there's pressure on nerves and
tendons and you literally have to physically re-stretch these tendons and that requires physical
therapy, which could involve a variety of exercises and trigger point massage and so on.

The whole structure of the shoulder connective tissue gets screwed up. I had one shoulder treated 30
plus years ago. The worst shoulder, which was the right one, usually the dominant side is worse, so
the right one was treated and I gave that priority because it hurt a lot. I let the left one alone
for the time being and then my practice got so busy that I couldn't visit the physiotherapist.

He had no night hours and I didn't have time to see him during the day, so I left the left shoulder
alone and finally got around to working on it about a year ago and with that big a delay, it takes a
lot of time.

For a while, I couldn't sleep in certain positions because of the left shoulder. Now that's much
better and we have it almost completely cured, but it'll probably take and I'm not too diligent in
my exercising, doing the special exercises for it, so it could have been fixed perhaps faster.
Anyway, I have a little left in the left shoulder and the right shoulder is all fixed.

That's basically the story. It doesn't heal itself. If you have elevated blood sugars, it'll get
worse.

You get more glycation collagen. Collagen has a half-life in a young person of about 15 years and so
that means after 15 years, half the glycated collagen is still there. After 30 years, a quarter of
it is still there.
