### How to maintain a normal blood sugar while doing high-intensity interval training? 

Well, first of all, in my experience, interval training is not as high intensity as people think. I
watch the people at my gym who perform interval training running from one machine to another and
they don't get anywhere near the kind of cardiovascular exercise that I get when I go on an
elliptical machine and get my heart rate above my theoretical maximum. So interval training is, in
certain respects, phony. It's very hard to get your heart rate up really substantially.

But what you can do is have a continuous glucose monitor as I do. You can take frequent blood sugar
readings if you do or if you don't have a continuous glucose monitor and you could use liquid
glucose to bring your blood sugar up if it gets too low.

Now, I wear a continuous glucose monitor when I exercise. When I do a cardiovascular exercise, which
is totally separate from my weightlifting bodybuilding exercises, I'll get my blood sugar up a
little bit. I'll get it up to 90 before I start when my target is usually about 83.

It takes me about, I guess, a half an hour, maybe 40 minutes of going up to my maximum heart rate
several times to get what I consider an adequate workout. Over that period of time, my blood sugar
might drop considerably.

So in addition to having a continuous glucose monitor on, I will stop after I reach my maximum
twice. I'll get another blood sugar and correct it before I go after my theoretical maximum for the
third time. All the while, I'm using liquid glucose as the vehicle for raising my blood sugar.

Now, if you're doing interval training, you're going to have to take a break to check your blood
sugar. It depends how long you're doing the training.

If you're doing it for only 20 minutes, you might not have a tremendous drop. But if you're doing it
for a period of an hour or more, you may have to stop and check your blood sugar several times.

The continuous glucose monitor, remember, is usually a half an hour behind your real blood sugar.
