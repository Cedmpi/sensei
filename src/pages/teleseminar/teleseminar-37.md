### Aside from waiting 3 hours after getting up, are there any other time-related restrictions on exercise? Specifically, should one wait for a certain amount of time after eating meals? [short: Are there time restrictions on exercise after meals?]

Well, exercise can slow digestion on the one hand, but certain kinds of exercise can accelerate
digestion if you have gastroparesis. For example, I have one patient who I've treated for 20 years.
He came to me with gastroparesis and the only treatment it needed was for him to walk after meals.

So when he would walk after dinner, and it was only not after all meals, just after dinner,
digestion is always slowest at dinner. So he would walk every night after dinner if it weren't
raining and his blood sugars would be stable on the nights that he walked. When he didn't walk, he
would go low after the dinner and then high the next morning due to the gastroparesis.

So that's about all that I can tell you about exercise after meals. If you're feeling full and you
do not have gastroparesis, it probably is not a smart thing to exercise. You'll be more comfortable
if you wait until you've digested the meal.

Before I started going to an outside gym, I would do my exercise at home in my home gym and it would
always be after dinner. It was about an hour after dinner, maybe two hours, and I never had any
trouble doing that.

I do my cardiac exercise at home on an elliptical machine and I do that about an hour and a half
after dinner. I don't have any problem with it.
