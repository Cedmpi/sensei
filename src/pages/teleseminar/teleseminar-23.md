### Special subject: Effects of dietary protein on body weight

Our special subject for today is going to be the effects of dietary protein on body
weight.

The reason I bring up this subject is because a number of the questions, both this month
and in prior months, advocate the elimination of protein for the purpose of reducing insulin
requirements and losing weight.

This is obese people start cutting out protein.
Big, big mistake.

I think I've gone into this before, but I cannot overemphasize the importance of maintaining
protein nutrition.
Every individual needs a minimum amount of protein to survive.
You need protein for your muscles, protein for your organs, et cetera, even for hormones.
You cannot survive without an adequate amount of protein.

I've seen people come in here who were boasting that they were on ketogenic diets where they
were eating a lot of fat to keep their blood sugars down, and they had wasted away and
they were generally in poor health.

If you want to lose weight, you cut down the carbohydrate.
We know that insulin is an anabolic hormone, it builds both fat and muscle.
It requires carbohydrate in order to build fat, requires protein in order to build muscle.

If you reduce the carbohydrate, the insulin will be unable to build fat, but if you reduce
the protein, you'll be unable to maintain your muscle mass, and that's potentially dangerous.
In order to lose weight, you cut the carbohydrate.

The fat in your diet has relatively little effect on body fat because dietary fat has
very little effect on insulin requirements, whether you're a non-diabetic and make your
own insulin or whether you're a type 1 diabetic who injects insulin.

The amount of fat you eat has a minimal effect on how much insulin your body needs, but the
amount of carbohydrate you eat has a tremendous effect on the amount of insulin your body
needs, and insulin, as I said, can build fat.

If you want to lose weight, you cut the carbohydrate.
If you want to gain weight, in theory, you could add carbohydrate, but the trouble is
that you will have a much bigger effect on blood sugar if you add carbohydrate than if
you add insulin, than if you add protein, and the dietary proteins effect on blood sugar,
the timing of the protein more likely matches the timing of injected regular insulin to
cover the meal.

If you want to gain weight, you increase your dietary protein, cover it with regular insulin
at meal time, and that is the technique that I usually use.
