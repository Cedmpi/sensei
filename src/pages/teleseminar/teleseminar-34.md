### Special subject: Beta cell replication research in Diabetologia

There was an article published in the May 18th version of Diabetologia, and its title is [Advances in
Drug Discovery for Human Beta Cell Replication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6239977/). This is a summary of all of the published studies
that are in process for reversing diabetes by causing beta cell replication. Beta cells are the
cells that make insulin, and what it points out is that the most advanced of these studies are still
many years away from successful testing in animals and even further years away from testing in
humans. My guess for after reading this was that for a drug cure for diabetes, we're at least 10
years away from successful trials on humans.

So if you hear of someone today making claims of a cure, selling a supplement or vitamin or other
chemical that supposedly cures diabetes, you can be pretty sure that it's a rip off. These claims
are constantly being made. If you were to search on the internet for a cure for diabetes, you'd
probably find more than a dozen of them, and none of them work. There are even reports of studies by
legitimate research facilities, prominent universities, etc., claiming cures, and likewise, these
things have not been replicated or successful elsewhere.

### Do you have patients in your practice who have type 1 diabetes and take metformin? What are the reasons for starting or stopping metformin for these patients? [short: Using metformin for type 1 diabetes?]

Well, in general, I do have many type 1 diabetics who take metformin, and it has to do with insulin
resistance. Metformin appears to fight insulin resistance by several mechanisms, and when would a
type 1 diabetic have insulin resistance?

Well, first of all, if they're overweight, and nowadays, there are occurring more and more
overweight type 1s because the powers that be are recommending high carbohydrate diets, which, and
high insulin doses to cover the carbohydrate, and this builds fat, so diabetics are being made fat
by the guidelines. So there are many obese type 1 diabetics, and you could reduce the insulin
resistance caused by the obesity with the help of metformin.

Then there's the condition known as polycystic ovarian syndrome, or PCOS, which affects supposedly
between 5 and 15 percent of women, and this responds quite nicely to metformin, and we use it for
them. Many of those people are diabetic and have been classified as type 1 diabetics. Then there are
women who, during their menstrual periods, or usually the week before the menstrual period, have
erratic blood sugars, and that condition seems to respond to metformin also.

By the way, when I say metformin, I really mean glucophage, the brand name, because I've found in my
patients that glucophage is much more potent than the generic versions.

Then we have children with growth spurts that become insulin resistant while they're growing, and we
give them glucophage, and they require less insulin, and their blood sugars are more level.

Then people who have been given a shot of prednisone or any steroid tend to have high blood sugars.
It could last for sometimes several months from one injection, like a doctor injects prednisone into
a joint and tells you that their blood sugars will be okay after a few days, and it really takes a
few months. Well, we give those people metformin, and that seems to help lower the insulin
resistance there.

Why would we stop giving a patient metformin? There are two situations. One, if it doesn't work, if
it doesn't do the job. Another situation might be if it causes vitamin B12 malabsorption.

Now, one can get around this by giving patients calcium pills. The metformin seems to be absorbed at
sites, competes with B12 for the same sites on the intestines, and some people develop anemia from
poor B12 absorption from the diet, and it seems that if you give a patient a calcium pill like a
Tums with every dose of metformin, you can get around this.

But I've not seen this for real. I've only read about it. I have many patients taking metformin, and
none of them have either had to take B12 or Tums. There are tests to see if you get B12
malabsorption, and I've discussed that in my teleseminars.
