### Special subject: Flu prevention with Sambucus

Before we get into our questions, we have a few special subjects I'd like to cover. One has to do
with the flu, which is now pretty widespread in the USA and probably around the world. Those of you
who have read my books know that I've been recommending Sambucus, an extract of the black elderberry
tree, for prevention of influenza infections.

It doesn't work 100%, but it works. In one study, about 70% of the users did not get the flu.

I recommend that when people are exposed to the flu that they start a treatment with Sambucus. It
comes in lozenges and a liquid. For the least carbohydrate for diabetics, I recommend the lozenges.
You suck the lozenges.

The dosing is 2 lozenges every 6 hours within 3 hours of initial exposure or infection.

I extend that and say if you think you might be exposed, you start taking it. If you're going to a
theater or going on an airplane, even if it's not flu season, cold spread in the airplanes very
rapidly and it's a good idea to take 2 lozenges every 6 hours of your [flying](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27023596/).

I see mistakes in the use of the Sambucus very frequently.

Just today, I had a phone call from a lady patient whose blood sugars have gone up. Her children
came home from school with the sniffles, as she called it. She's been taking one Sambucus twice a
day all winter in anticipation of possible exposure.

But here, the kids came home sick. They eventually developed the flu and she had real exposure.

For that, you need 2 tablets, 2 lozenges every 6 hours. She didn't up her dose. She now has a
congested throat and a fever, so it happened pretty rapidly.

The game plan is if you're exposed, you take the full dose. If you're thinking of being exposed,
like going to a group meeting in the wintertime, take the full dose.

Being cautious in the wintertime to take one or two a day is okay, but don't avoid taking the full
dose when you're exposed.

### Special subject: Extreme dieting and bone loss

The next little subject is a [study of obese postmenopausal women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31664441/) who were put on diets to lose
weight, reduce the chance of diabetes, etc. They were on two different diets that were studied. One
was an extreme diet of only 800 calories a day and the other was a more moderate diet.

The people on both diets lost weight. Those on the extreme diet lost more weight, but they also had
very significant bone loss.

Eventually, when they went back to their old diets, they regained the weight, but the women on the
extreme diet did not regain the bone.

That reminds me of what I've been warning about people who endure fasting to treat their diabetes
where there are all kinds of fasting regimens like every other day, every other week, etc. These
people lose fat and muscle while they're fasting and later on when they restore their usual meals,
they gain back the fat but not the muscle. Now we see that even can extend to bone in postmenopausal
women.

### Special subject: Blood sugar variability and the brain

We have another study from the December issue of Diabetes Care, which is published by the American
Diabetes Association, and it reiterates something that I've been saying for years.

In this [particular study, type 1 young adults were studied](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article-abstract/42/12/2334/36183/Visit-to-Visit-Fasting-Glucose-Variability-in?redirectedFrom=fulltext) who would appear in clinic and what they
found was they would check a blood sugar on arrival at the clinic. The greater the variation in
blood sugar from one visit to the next, the more damage to the hippocampus of the brain was found.

Highly variable blood sugars, which by the way are associated usually with high hemoglobin A1Cs,
that was a study that there were two studies that I presented at prior teleseminars. We know that
people with higher hemoglobin A1Cs tend to have higher blood sugar variability.

Now this study of high blood sugar variability leads to damage to a particular site in the brain
called the hippocampus, which is responsible for the conversion of short-term memory to long-term
memory, also for navigation, where you located geographically.

People who get lost driving their car or riding their bike may have hippocampal damage, and there
are certainly additional functions of the hippocampus, but this is just reiterating the effect of
blood sugar on the brain.

Now even though this was published in the Journal of the American Diabetes Association, the American
Diabetes Association still recommends high blood sugars for type 1 diabetics, especially young ones.  
It's an endless battle.

Now we're going to get down to our questions.

Before we start the questions, I want to remind you that the answers I give are generic answers.

### Recently diagnosed type 1 daughter, age 14, do you recommend the Dexcom monitor? ### I take nighttime Lantus, which is extremely painful. The endocrinologist prescribed 22 units. It gives me so much pain, what do you recommend I do? [short: Painful Lantus injections?]

Well, Lantus insulin must be kept at a low pH, that is it is acidic. It has a pH of 4, whereas the
normal pH of a body is about 7.3, so it's quite acidic.

Why does it have to be acidic? Because when it is in an acid state, it is not readily soluble in the
fat in the body and dissolves slowly.

The body pH changes that acidity, makes it less acid, so it becomes more soluble over time. This is
how the time release effect is generated, by taking an acid solution and gradually making it neutral
just by exposure to the body.

Acid can be painful. They actually add hydrochloric acid to the neutral solution when they make it
in order to make it acid and a number of people find that it burns.

The game plan should be to switch from Lantus to another insulin. Probably the best insulin to
switch to would be Tresiba, which is longer acting. There's also Levemir.

Another problem with Lantus insulin, which I've pointed out, is that it has 165 times the affinity
for IGF-1 receptors than human insulin does. The IGF-1 receptors are prevalent in cancer tissue and
you can make cancer cells replicate by stimulating IGF-1 receptors, so Lantus has an increased
cancer risk.

The dose 22 units is a large dose and it could be that you may not need it all to be given in one
shot at nighttime.

Most of the shots that I give of long-acting insulin or that I prescribe are twice daily smaller
doses. I take 3 units of Tresiba at about 4 a.m. and at bedtime. Of course, the dose does depend
upon how much insulin you may be making yourself and also upon your body weight.

First of all, there are many people who experience the symptoms of a low blood sugar when they're

Another possibility, since you speak of dizziness and usually when people speak of dizziness,

Furthermore, since he or she is having blood sugars as high as 180, they may be dehydrated. When
your blood sugar is that high, you peel away glucose and you can easily become dehydrated.
Dehydration can lead to low blood pressure and what's called near syncope.

There are many options as to what may be going on. Of course, real, true low blood sugars can cause
discomfort and make you feel like you might pass out.

I suggest you read the chapter in my book, Diabetes Solution, about hypoglycemia. In fact, you could

### I crave carbohydrates. I eat when I am bored and tired. I'm contemplating Ozempic as a solution. [short: Can Ozempic help with carbohydrate cravings?]

Ozempic is a once a week GLP-1 agonist. GLP-1 agonists increase the body's production of amylin,
which is a satiety hormone. This is a person who is an overeater and is wisely looking to a GLP-1
agonist to increase his or her satiety.

The problem is that if you're 24 hours a day all week long, which is the action of Ozempic, you're
going to rapidly become tolerant of the effect. Most people who overeat overeat at certain times of
the day. Sometimes they overeat in the late afternoon. Most commonly, it's after dinner.

We can take a rapid-acting GLP-1 agonist and take it so that it's working when you're going to be
craving the food. That's more likely to work, whereas if you're giving it all day long, day after
day, it's not going to work, at least in my experience. I've had a few patients who wanted to try it
and had no effect on appetite whatsoever.

Now, this person also says his or her A1c is 7.0, which is high, so all the more urgency to
interfere with your overeating. If you read my book, Diabetes Solution, we have some suggestions for
curbing overeating. It's not an easy situation to cover.

### My last lipid panel revealed LDL of 172 was 94, 6 months ago, HDL 98 versus 88 and triglycerides of 46 versus 35. Is this expected on a low-carbohydrate diet? My cardiologist wants me on a statin to lower the LDL, which I am not thrilled to do but would need arguments. [short: Should I take a statin for high LDL on a low-carb diet?]

Total LDL has not been found to be a predictor of a heart disease or other diseases. LDL particle
size has been found as a predictor. Small dense LDL is more likely to infiltrate the walls of
arteries.

There are other reasonable predictors. Triglyceride is a very good predictor, and your triglyceride
is low. It's even come down lower.

There are other tests that can be done. I would suggest that you ask your cardiologist to update
himself on the scientific literature for cardiac risk factors that can be measured in the blood.

I can list a few of them for you. There's LDL particle size. There's fibrinogen. There's C-reactive
protein.

Most important, especially if you're female, is lipoprotein(a). In my experience,
every patient who's come to see me for the first time where we get a load of blood tests, everyone
who's had an elevated Lp(a) has already had a heart attack.

That tends to be elevated in women who have low estrogen levels, and you could bring the Lp(a)
way down by estrogen replacement.

Another test that your cardiologist may want to do, which is in a sense more invasive, is an imaging
test for a coronary artery calcium score. You don't impose a drastic treatment like statins based
solely upon an LDL.

### Is there a link between type 1 diabetes and osteoporosis?

We should say there's a link between elevated blood sugars and osteoporosis.

When I was about 20 years old, I had broken a pinky and I went to the emergency room. They x-rayed

There is a link, but the link is not between diabetes and osteoporosis, but between high blood
sugars and osteoporosis.

### Human creatinine ratio results are 16.2. What does this mean?

This is reported on a routine chem screen or chemical profile of the blood. They usually do two
tests that reflect kidney function. One is the serum creatinine, which creatinine is a breakdown
product of muscle tissue that appears in the serum and is cleared out of the blood by the kidneys
into the urine. The blood urea nitrogen is a measure of urea in the blood that is also cleared away

You're looking at two things that are cleared away by the kidneys. There's a ratio of one to the
