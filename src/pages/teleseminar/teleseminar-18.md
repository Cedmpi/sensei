### Special subject: Using insulin pens

The first relates to the use of insulin pens.
Now insulin pens have advantages and disadvantages.
The advantage is that you don't have to go through the trouble of filling the syringe
from a vial.

A disadvantage is that you cannot get half-unit measurements with insulin syringes, and for
most children, you need half-unit measurements.
Another problem is that you cannot get diluted insulin through insulin pens.
Those are two problems.

The third problem is that the needle is very short, I believe about 6 mm, so sometimes
you may get leakage from such a shallow injection.
Most people do not use insulin pens properly.
In particular, I'm thinking about how long you'll keep the needle in your skin holding
down the button, and then later when you release the button.

What I find is that I have to hold the button down during an injection.
When I throw in the needle, then when the needle is fully into my skin, I push the button
down and I hold it down for 10 seconds, but seconds are hard to count.

What I do is I count to 20, and then after you release the button, you have to leave
it in for another 10 seconds, so I count to 20 again slowly.

Even then, sometimes I get leakage, but at least I'll do much better by doing those two
20-second intervals rather than pulling it out rapidly.
As I said, most people don't wait, as I explained.

The next problem that I want to discuss, subject number two, relates to taking insulin when
